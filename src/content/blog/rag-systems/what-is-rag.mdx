## The Open-Book Exam Analogy

A plain LLM is like a closed-book exam - it can only use what it memorized during training. RAG is an open-book exam - the model retrieves relevant context from external documents before generating an answer. This means it can answer questions about your private data, stay current, and cite sources.

The RAG pipeline has two distinct phases: an offline phase (index your documents) and an online phase (answer user queries). Both matter equally - a perfect LLM with bad retrieval produces bad answers.

## The 3 Problems RAG Solves

1. **Knowledge cutoff** - LLMs don't know about events after their training date. RAG retrieves current information from your knowledge base.
2. **Hallucination** - grounding answers in retrieved documents reduces fabrication. The LLM can only state facts that appear in the context.
3. **Private data** - your company's internal docs, Confluence pages, Slack history, and proprietary databases aren't in the training data. RAG makes them accessible.

The quality bottleneck is almost always retrieval. If the right chunks aren't retrieved, even GPT-4 can't generate a correct answer. This is why search engineering skills are critical for RAG systems.

## Key Takeaway

RAG = Retrieve relevant context + Augment the prompt with that context + Generate an answer. The retrieval quality is the bottleneck - invest in chunking, embedding model selection, and hybrid search before tuning the LLM prompt.
