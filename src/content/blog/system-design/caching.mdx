## The Librarian's Desk

Imagine a librarian who keeps the most requested books on their desk instead of walking to the stacks. When someone asks for "Harry Potter," it's right there (cache hit, ~0.1ms). When someone asks for an obscure book, the librarian walks to the shelves (cache miss, ~50-200ms). The desk has limited space, so the librarian removes the book that hasn't been asked for the longest time. That's LRU caching.

Caching is the single most impactful optimization for search latency and cost. A well-designed cache can reduce search engine load by 40-60%.

## What to Cache in Search

Not all data is equal. Cache the expensive-to-compute, frequently-accessed layers:

| Cache Layer | What | TTL | Hit Rate Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Query result cache** | Full serialized response for (query, filters, sort, page) | 5-15 min | 30-50% |
| **Filter cache** | Pre-computed bitsets for common filters (brand, in_stock) | 5 min | 80-95% |
| **Embedding cache** | Pre-computed query embeddings | 24 hours | 20-40% |
| **Autocomplete cache** | Suggestions for popular prefixes | 1 hour | 60-80% |
| **Field data cache** | Uninverted field values for sorting/aggregations | Until evicted | ~100% |

Effective QPS on ES = Total QPS × (1 - Cache Hit Ratio). At 40% cache hit ratio: 10K QPS × 0.6 = 6K QPS reaching Elasticsearch.

## LRU vs LFU vs W-TinyLFU

| Strategy | Best For | Weakness | Complexity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LRU** | General-purpose, changing workloads | Frequency-blind - one-time queries evict popular ones | O(1) |
| **LFU** | Stable popularity distribution | Slow to adapt - once-popular items clog the cache | O(log n) |
| **W-TinyLFU** | Production caches (Caffeine) | Requires tuning window size | Sketch-based |

W-TinyLFU (used by Caffeine in the JVM world) combines a small LRU admission window with frequency-based eviction. It adapts to workload shifts while maintaining near-optimal hit rates.

## Cache Invalidation and Zipf's Law

Cache invalidation strategies: TTL-based (simple, bounded staleness), event-driven (Kafka consumer invalidates on product change), tag-based (tag entries with product IDs, invalidate all containing a changed product), and write-through (update cache simultaneously with index).

Zipf's law makes caching powerful: the frequency of the n-th most common query is proportional to 1/n. The top 1% of queries account for ~30% of traffic. The top 10K queries cover ~60-70% of traffic. Caching these gives disproportionate benefit.

Always warm the cache after deployments, restarts, or reindexing. Pre-load the top 10K queries from yesterday's analytics to avoid cold-start latency spikes.

## Key Takeaway

Cache popular queries aggressively (Zipf's law means a small cache handles most traffic). Layer caches: Client → CDN → Application (Redis) → Search Engine internal. Use TTL + event-driven invalidation as a hybrid. Warm caches proactively after deploys. Effective Latency = (1 - hit_rate) × miss_latency + hit_rate × hit_latency.
